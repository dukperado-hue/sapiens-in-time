S3587	 The Indus cities did not fall in a day. The monsoon weakened and the rivers shifted slowly over centuries, so slowly that hardly anyone saw a "collapse" within one life, only a little less rain, one more dry well. Those who adapted, changing crops, moving, splitting into small villages, survived and became ancestors of South Asians today. What vanished was the great system built for the old world: the cities, the seals and the writing. Scholars still argue how much nature was to blame, but the lesson still holds for a world whose climate is changing today.
