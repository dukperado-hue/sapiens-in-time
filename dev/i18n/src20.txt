S6810	Around 1450 BCE most Minoan centres were destroyed and Mycenaeans from the mainland took control of Knossos. The palace tablets switched from Linear A, still unread, to Linear B, an early form of Greek deciphered in 1952.

S6815	The Lion Gate
S6816	At Mycenae workers heave stones weighing many tons into the walls, and set above the gate a slab carved with two lions facing a column.
S6817	The Lion Gate of Mycenae, around 1250 BCE, is the oldest large sculpture in Europe still standing where it was made. Later Greeks thought only the giant Cyclopes could have lifted such stones ✦, and the walls are still called “Cyclopean”.
S6818	Help push stones up the ramp
S6819	No giants needed: just enough men, rope and beer.
S6820	Watch the lions being carved

S6822	The great ship that never came home
S6823	A great merchant ship leaves harbour carrying ten tons of copper in oxhide-shaped ingots, a ton of tin, blue glass, ebony, ivory and gold. A month later word comes that it struck a rocky cape and sank.
S6824	The Uluburun wreck, around 1300 BCE, was found off Turkey in 1982 by a sponge diver. Its cargo came from many cultures, including a gold scarab bearing Queen Nefertiti’s name, evidence of how widely Bronze Age trade linked the world. Ten parts copper to one of tin is the recipe for bronze.
S6825	Help load the copper ingots
S6826	Each ingot weighs as much as you. Three thousand years later, archaeologists will lift it from the seabed.
S6827	Mourn the sailors
S6828	There is no grave to visit, only the sea, looking as calm as ever.
S6829	A letter to a brother
S6830	Envoys return from the Hittite lands in Anatolia with a clay letter from the Hittite king. In it, one of the greatest kings in the world calls your king “my brother”, then complains about a raider hiding in your lands.
S6831	Several Hittite royal letters mention a land called Ahhiyawa, which most scholars identify with the Mycenaean Greeks. In the Tawagalawa letter of around 1250 BCE the Hittite king calls the king of Ahhiyawa his brother and asks him to hand over a raider named Piyamaradu.
S6832	Feel proud of your king
S6833	Your king puts on airs for a month. So do the taxes.
S6834	Ask who the raider is
S6835	No one knows. Or everyone knows and won’t say.
S6836	The city on the straits
S6837	The warriors of your town are gathering ships to raid Wilusa, the high-walled city on the straits to the east. The leaders promise gold and captives. The old men say they will bring back only bodies.
S6838	Hittite records mention a city called Wilusa, which most scholars identify with Troy (Ilios in Greek). Troy VII was destroyed around 1180 BCE. The Trojan War of Homer’s epic, with its wooden horse and Helen, is legend ✦; how much of it happened no one knows.
S6839	Join the raid
S6840	An arrow from the walls takes you in the thigh. You come home empty-handed and limp for the rest of your life.
S6841	You come back with one copper cup and a story that grows longer every year.
S6842	Half of those who went never return. Those who do tell stories until they become songs.
S6843	The enemy ships have come
S6844	In the palace a scribe writes the king’s letter to the king of Cyprus: “My father, behold, the enemy’s ships came; my cities were burned, and they did evil things in my country. All my troops are far away in the Hittite land.”
S6845	Ugarit was destroyed around 1185 BCE in the Bronze Age collapse. This letter was found in the palace archives; the popular story that the tablets were still in a kiln, waiting to be sent when the city fell, is a misunderstanding passed down ✦. Ugarit also gave us one of the oldest alphabets, written in cuneiform signs.
S6846	Help carry the letter to the harbour
S6847	The sea is full of strange sails. You are not sure the messenger ship can get out.
S6848	Escaped Ugarit before it fell
S6849	From the hills you watch smoke rise from a city no one will ever rebuild.
S6850	The fire that saved the words
S6851	The palace of Pylos goes up in flames. The archive room, full of clay tablets listing sheep, oil and rowers, grows so hot that the unbaked tablets are fired as hard as pottery.
S6852	About a thousand Linear B tablets survive because fire swept the palace of Pylos around 1180 BCE; clay records were normally temporary and unbaked. Some of the last tablets mention sending rowers to guard the coast, as if danger were known to be coming.
S6853	Help fight the fire
S6854	The fire is too strong. All you can do is get people out.
S6855	Flee to the mountains
S6856	Survived the burning of Pylos
S6857	The world of the palaces ends in a night. Life in the mountain villages goes on.
S6858	News from Egypt
S6859	A trader says Pharaoh Ramesses III has defeated the “Sea Peoples” at the mouths of the Nile and had the battles on land and water carved on the walls of a great temple. Some survivors have fled back to your coast.
S6860	The reliefs at Medinet Habu, around 1177 BCE, show the oldest surviving picture of a sea battle. “Sea Peoples” is a modern scholars’ name for several groups on the move by sea during the Bronze Age collapse, who were invaders and refugees at once.
S6861	Feed the survivors
S6862	They speak a tongue you don’t know, but hunger is understood in every language.
S6863	Drive them from the harbour
S6864	Their boats put to sea again. You don’t know where they will land.
S6865	The biggest bronze
S6866	Hundreds of casters pour molten bronze from dozens of crucibles at once into a clay mould the size of a room, making a four-legged cauldron for the late mother of the king.
S6867	The Houmuwu (or Simuwu) ding, weighing 832.84 kg, is the largest known Shang bronze, made around the 13th–12th century BCE. Villagers dug it up in 1939 and reburied it to hide it from Japanese soldiers during the war. It is now in the National Museum of China.
S6868	Help work the bellows
S6869	Half your eyebrows are singed, but the vessel comes out perfect.
S6870	Count the crucibles
S6871	You count seventy before everything turns to smoke and light.
S6872	Burying the masks
S6873	The people of the river city dig great pits, break and burn bronze masks with bulging eyes, bronze heads, hundreds of elephant tusks and a bronze tree taller than a man, then bury it all. No one tells you why.
S6874	The pits at Sanxingdui in Sichuan were found in 1986, and six more in 2019–2022. New dating suggests they were filled around 1100 BCE. The culture was very different from the Shang and left no writing; why they smashed and buried such treasures is still a mystery.
S6875	Help carry the tusks
S6876	The tusks are heavy and cold. You feel you are burying something bigger than things.
S6877	Secretly keep a little scrap of gold foil
S6878	You hide it all your life and never dare show anyone.
S6879	A lake of wine, a forest of meat
S6880	Rumour from the palace says King Di Xin has dug a pool filled with wine deep enough to row a boat in, and hung meat from the trees like a forest, where naked guests chase each other all night.
S6881	The tale of the “lake of wine and forest of meat” of Di Xin, last Shang king, was written by later historians drawing on the stories of the victorious Zhou ✦. There may be some truth in it, but winners tend to paint losers as monsters to justify taking power.
S6882	Believe the rumour
S6883	You pass it on, and the story grows each time.
S6884	Wonder who is spreading it
S6885	Most of the tellers have just come back from the west, where the Zhou are gathering strength.
S6886	The prisoner who drew lines
S6887	Ji Chang, Lord of the West and head of the Zhou, has been imprisoned by the Shang king at Youli. People say that in prison he lays out sticks in whole and broken lines, arranging them into sixty-four figures of six lines each.
S6888	Chinese tradition says King Wen of Zhou arranged the 64 hexagrams of the Yijing (I Ching) while imprisoned ✦. Modern scholars think the book grew out of early Zhou divination over a long time, not from one man.
S6889	Smuggle food in to him

S6891	Try laying out the sticks yourself
S6892	You get to ten figures and lose track. Three thousand years later, people around the world still consult this book.

S6895	The story of Bo Yi and Shu Qi is told by the historian Sima Qian, writing about a thousand years later ✦. The brothers starved on the mountain and became models of loyalty in Chinese culture; Confucius praised them.

S6900	A mountain of earth
S6901	On an island in the swamps thousands of people carry clay in baskets, heaping it into a mound higher than any tree in the forest. The leaders say it is a sacred mountain made by human hands.
S6902	The Great Pyramid of La Venta is built of clay, about 30 m tall, one of the oldest pyramids in Mesoamerica, around 900–400 BCE. It may imitate the volcanoes of the nearby Tuxtla mountains.

S6908	Craftsmen lay hundreds of green serpentine blocks into a huge jaguar-face mosaic on the ground. It takes months. When it is finished, everyone helps cover it with earth until nothing shows, and no one sees it again.
S6909	At La Venta archaeologists found “massive offerings”: thousands of tons of serpentine blocks buried underground, and mosaic pavements of animal faces covered over as soon as they were finished. Burying such wealth may have been an offering to the underworld.
