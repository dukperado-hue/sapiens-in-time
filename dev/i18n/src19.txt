S5557	At its height Rome may have held about a million people, more than any Western city until the 19th century. The poor lived in tall rented blocks called insulae, often without kitchens; fires were common, and the higher the floor, the cheaper the rent, because it was harder to escape.
S5558	Pompeii was a middling town of perhaps 10,000–20,000 people. Vesuvius erupted in 79 CE and buried it in ash, giving us the most detailed picture of everyday Roman life, from loaves in the ovens to the messages people scrawled on walls.
